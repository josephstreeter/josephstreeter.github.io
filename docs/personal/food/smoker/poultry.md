## Smoked Whole Chicken

A whole chicken, dry-brined and smoked hot enough to crisp the skin. Cook it whole, or spatchcock it (remove the backbone and flatten it) so it cooks faster and more evenly.

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **Cooker** | Smoker |
| **Prep time** | 15 minutes, plus 4–24 hours to dry brine |
| **Cook time** | 1½–2 hours whole, 1–1¼ hours spatchcocked |
| **Servings** | 4 |
| **Cook temp** | 300–325°F |
| **Target internal temp** | 160–165°F in the breast, 175°F in the thigh |
| **Wood** | Cherry, apple, or pecan |

### Ingredients

**Chicken:**

- 1 whole chicken (4–5 lb), giblets removed
- 2 tablespoons olive oil or melted butter
- Kitchen twine (optional, for a whole bird)
- Sharp poultry shears (for spatchcocking)

**Dry brine:**

- 1 tablespoon Diamond Crystal kosher salt, or 1½ teaspoons Morton kosher salt
- 1 teaspoon baking powder (not baking soda), for crisper skin

**Rub:**

- Your favorite poultry rub, or a mix of:
  - 1 tablespoon smoked paprika
  - 1 tablespoon brown sugar
  - 1 teaspoon black pepper
  - 1 teaspoon garlic powder
  - 1 teaspoon onion powder
  - ½ teaspoon dried thyme

### Instructions

1. **Spatchcock (optional).** Set the chicken breast side down. Cut along both sides of the backbone with poultry shears and remove it. Flip the bird and press down firmly on the breastbone to flatten it.
2. **Dry brine.** Pat the chicken dry. Mix the salt and baking powder and sprinkle it evenly over all sides. Refrigerate uncovered on a rack for 4–24 hours.
3. **Season.** Brush the chicken lightly with oil or melted butter and coat it with the rub. For a whole bird, tuck the wing tips under the back and tie the legs together.
4. **Preheat the smoker** to 300–325°F with cherry, apple, or pecan wood.
5. **Smoke** breast side up (skin side up if spatchcocked). Put a probe in the thickest part of the breast, not touching bone.
6. **Finish.** Pull the chicken when the breast reaches 160–165°F and the thigh reaches at least 175°F.
7. **Rest** for 10–15 minutes, then carve.

### Notes

- Cherry wood gives the skin a deep mahogany color.
- If the skin isn't crisp, finish the chicken over direct high heat for a few minutes per side, skin side down last.

## Smoked Chicken Wings and Thighs

Wings and bone-in thighs, smoked low for flavor and then finished hot to crisp the skin. Both are dark meat (or close to it), so they're better cooked past 165°F, where the connective tissue softens.

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **Cooker** | Smoker |
| **Prep time** | 15 minutes, plus 1–24 hours to dry brine |
| **Cook time** | About 1¼ hours for wings, 1½ hours for thighs |
| **Servings** | 4–6 |
| **Cook temp** | 250°F, then 400°F to finish |
| **Target internal temp** | 175–185°F |
| **Wood** | Apple, cherry, or hickory |

### Ingredients

**Chicken:**

- 3 lb chicken wings, split into flats and drums, tips removed
- 3 lb bone-in, skin-on chicken thighs (about 8)

**Dry brine:**

- 2 tablespoons Diamond Crystal kosher salt, or 1 tablespoon Morton kosher salt
- 2 teaspoons baking powder (not baking soda), for crisper skin

**Rub:**

- Your favorite poultry rub, or the rub from [Smoked Whole Chicken](#smoked-whole-chicken), doubled

**Buffalo sauce (optional, for wings):**

- ½ cup hot sauce, such as Frank's RedHot
- 4 tablespoons (½ stick) butter, melted
- 1 teaspoon Worcestershire sauce

### Instructions

1. **Dry brine.** Pat the wings and thighs dry. Toss them with the salt and baking powder and refrigerate uncovered on a rack for 1–24 hours.
2. **Season.** Coat everything with the rub.
3. **Preheat the smoker** to 250°F.
4. **Smoke** skin side up for 45 minutes to 1 hour.
5. **Crisp.** Raise the smoker to 400°F (or move the chicken to a hot grill) and cook until the skin is crisp and the meat reaches 175–185°F, about 20–30 minutes for wings and 30–40 minutes for thighs.
6. **Sauce (optional).** Whisk the buffalo sauce ingredients together and toss the wings in it while they're hot.
7. **Rest** for 5 minutes before serving.

### Notes

- Wings and thighs finish at different times. Start the thighs 15–20 minutes before the wings, or pull each as it's done.
- If your smoker can't reach 400°F, finish on a grill, under the broiler, or in an air fryer.

## Smoked Chicken Breast

Boneless, skinless chicken breasts, briefly brined so they stay juicy, then smoked low. Chicken breast is very lean, so pull it as soon as it's done.

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **Cooker** | Smoker |
| **Prep time** | 10 minutes, plus 1–2 hours to brine |
| **Cook time** | 1–1½ hours |
| **Servings** | 4 |
| **Cook temp** | 225–250°F |
| **Target internal temp** | 160°F, rising to 165°F while resting |
| **Wood** | Apple or cherry |

### Ingredients

**Chicken:**

- 4 boneless, skinless chicken breasts (about 8 oz each)
- 1 tablespoon olive oil

**Brine:**

- 4 cups cold water
- ¼ cup Diamond Crystal kosher salt, or 2 tablespoons Morton kosher salt
- 2 tablespoons brown sugar

**Rub:**

- Your favorite poultry rub, or the rub from [Smoked Whole Chicken](#smoked-whole-chicken)

**Glaze (optional):**

- ½ cup barbecue sauce

### Instructions

1. **Even out the thickness.** If the breasts are much thicker at one end, cover them with plastic wrap and pound the thick end to about 1 inch so they cook evenly.
2. **Brine.** Stir the salt and sugar into the water until dissolved. Add the chicken and refrigerate for 1–2 hours. Don't go longer, or the texture turns spongy.
3. **Season.** Remove the chicken, pat it dry, brush it with oil, and coat it with the rub.
4. **Preheat the smoker** to 225–250°F with apple or cherry wood.
5. **Smoke** until the chicken reaches 150°F, about 45 minutes to 1¼ hours.
6. **Glaze (optional).** Brush on the barbecue sauce and smoke until the chicken reaches 160°F, about 10–15 more minutes.
7. **Rest** for 5–10 minutes, loosely tented with foil. Check that it reaches 165°F before slicing.

### Notes

- Smoked chicken breast keeps well for meal prep, salads, and sandwiches. Refrigerate it within 2 hours of cooking.
- For a seared crust, finish each breast over direct high heat for 1–2 minutes per side.

## Smoked Whole Turkey

A whole turkey, dry-brined overnight, rubbed with herb butter under the skin, and smoked at a moderate temperature so the skin renders. The breast and the thighs cook at different rates, so check both temperatures.

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **Cooker** | Smoker |
| **Prep time** | 30 minutes, plus 24–48 hours to dry brine |
| **Cook time** | 3–4 hours for a 12–14 lb turkey |
| **Servings** | 10–12 |
| **Cook temp** | 275–300°F |
| **Target internal temp** | 165°F in the breast, 175°F in the thigh |
| **Wood** | Apple, cherry, or pecan |

### Ingredients

**Turkey:**

- 1 whole turkey (12–14 lb), fully thawed, neck and giblets removed
- Kitchen twine (optional, for tying the legs)

**Dry brine:**

- ¼ cup kosher salt (Diamond Crystal), or 3 tablespoons Morton kosher salt
- 1 tablespoon brown sugar
- 1 tablespoon black pepper
- 2 teaspoons garlic powder
- 1 teaspoon baking powder (not baking soda), for crisper skin

**Herb butter:**

- 1 stick (½ cup) unsalted butter, softened
- 3 cloves garlic, minced
- 1 tablespoon fresh rosemary, chopped
- 1 tablespoon fresh thyme leaves
- 1 tablespoon fresh sage, chopped
- 1 teaspoon smoked paprika

**Cavity aromatics (optional):**

- 1 onion, quartered
- 1 lemon, quartered
- A few sprigs each of rosemary, thyme, and sage

### Instructions

1. **Thaw.** Thaw a frozen turkey in the refrigerator, allowing about 24 hours for every 4–5 lb. A 12–14 lb turkey takes about 3 days.
2. **Dry brine.** Pat the turkey dry inside and out. Mix the dry brine ingredients and rub them all over the outside and inside the cavity, putting more on the breast and thighs. Set the turkey on a rack over a sheet pan and refrigerate it uncovered for 24–48 hours. Leaving it uncovered dries the skin so it crisps on the smoker.
3. **Make the herb butter.** Mix the butter, garlic, herbs, and paprika.
4. **Butter the turkey.** Loosen the skin over the breast and thighs with your fingers without tearing it. Push the herb butter under the skin and spread it evenly from the outside. Don't rinse the turkey first.
5. **Add aromatics.** Put the onion, lemon, and herb sprigs in the cavity if you're using them. Tuck the wing tips under the back and tie the legs together loosely.
6. **Preheat the smoker** to 275–300°F with apple, cherry, or pecan wood.
7. **Smoke** breast side up, with the thickest part of the bird toward the hottest side of the smoker. Put one probe in the thickest part of the breast and, if you have a second, one in the thickest part of the thigh, not touching bone.
8. **Check the temperature.** Start checking after about 2½ hours. Plan on roughly 15 minutes per pound at 275°F and less at 300°F.
9. **Finish.** Pull the turkey when the breast reaches 160–165°F and the thigh reaches at least 175°F. If the breast gets there first, cover it loosely with foil while the thighs finish.
10. **Rest** for 30–45 minutes, uncovered or loosely tented with foil. The breast will rise about 5°F while it rests.
11. **Carve.** Remove the legs and thighs first, then carve each breast off the bone in one piece and slice it across the grain.

### Notes

- **Don't stuff the turkey.** The stuffing slows the cook and keeps the middle of the bird in the danger zone too long. Cook the stuffing separately.
- **Low temperatures make rubbery skin.** At 225–250°F the skin stays soft. If the skin isn't brown and crisp when the bird is nearly done, raise the smoker to 325–350°F for the last 20–30 minutes.
- **Bigger birds:** turkeys over about 16 lb spend a long time warming through at smoker temperatures. Two smaller turkeys, or a spatchcocked turkey, cook more evenly.
- **Pulling at 160°F:** the USDA safe minimum for poultry is 165°F. Pulling the breast at 160°F only works if it rises to 165°F as it rests, so check it again before carving.
- **Drippings:** set a pan under the turkey to catch drippings for gravy. The smoke flavor in them is strong, so taste before using all of them.
- **Cook log:** record the date, weather, turkey weight, cook time, and changes here.

## Smoked Turkey Breast

A bone-in turkey breast, dry-brined with herb butter under the skin. It's a smaller cook than a whole turkey and has no thighs to wait on, so it's easier to get juicy.

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **Cooker** | Smoker |
| **Prep time** | 20 minutes, plus 12–24 hours to dry brine |
| **Cook time** | 2½–3½ hours for a 6–7 lb bone-in breast |
| **Servings** | 6–8 |
| **Cook temp** | 275°F |
| **Target internal temp** | 160°F, rising to 165°F while resting |
| **Wood** | Apple, cherry, or pecan |

### Ingredients

**Turkey:**

- 1 bone-in, skin-on turkey breast (6–7 lb), fully thawed

**Dry brine:**

- 2 tablespoons Diamond Crystal kosher salt, or 1 tablespoon Morton kosher salt
- 1 teaspoon black pepper
- 1 teaspoon garlic powder
- 1 teaspoon baking powder (not baking soda), for crisper skin

**Herb butter:**

- 4 tablespoons (½ stick) unsalted butter, softened
- 2 cloves garlic, minced
- 2 teaspoons fresh rosemary, chopped
- 2 teaspoons fresh thyme leaves
- 1 teaspoon smoked paprika

### Instructions

1. **Dry brine.** Pat the turkey breast dry. Mix the dry brine ingredients and rub them over the skin and the underside. Refrigerate uncovered on a rack for 12–24 hours.
2. **Butter.** Mix the herb butter ingredients. Loosen the skin with your fingers without tearing it, push the butter underneath, and spread it evenly from the outside.
3. **Preheat the smoker** to 275°F with apple, cherry, or pecan wood.
4. **Smoke** skin side up. Put a probe in the thickest part of the breast, not touching bone. Start checking after about 2 hours.
5. **Finish.** Pull the breast at 160°F. If the skin isn't crisp, raise the smoker to 325–350°F for the last 15–20 minutes.
6. **Rest** for 20–30 minutes, loosely tented with foil. Check that it reaches 165°F, then carve each side off the bone and slice across the grain.

### Notes

- **Boneless turkey breast** (3–4 lb) works too. Tie it into an even log with twine and expect about 1½–2 hours at 275°F.
- **Cook log:** record the date, weather, breast weight, cook time, and changes here.
